I am a PhD researcher in Computer Engineering at the University of California, San Diego, advised by Prof. Farinaz Koushanfar. My work focuses on **generative AI and agentic systems**, with an emphasis on trustworthy and efficient LLMs, code and hardware intelligence, and practical model adaptation.

My research spans **LLM safety and watermarking**, **agentic AI for hardware design and debugging**, **LLM post-training and adaptation**, and **efficient LLM inference**. I am particularly interested in building AI systems that remain useful under real deployment constraints: correctness, security, efficiency, tool interaction, and verifiability.

## Research Interests

- **Trustworthy & Secure LLMs** — watermarking, ownership verification, attestation, and robustness for generative models
- **Agentic AI for Hardware** — LLM agents for RTL generation, debugging, verification, and EDA-tool interaction
- **Model Adaptation & Post-Training** — SFT, RLHF/GRPO, PEFT, structured generation, and evaluation
- **Efficient LLM Systems** — KV-cache compression, inference efficiency, and deployment-aware model design
- **AI for Systems & Hardware** — code intelligence, hardware generation, distributed learning, and secure ML systems

## Current Research

### Code LLM Watermarking
I develop training-based watermarking methods for code LLMs that jointly optimize watermark detectability and functional correctness. My work includes reinforcement-learning-based watermarking and multi-bit watermarking for code generation.

### Agentic RTL Generation & Debugging
I work on agentic systems that combine language models with static analysis, simulators, and EDA tools to generate, debug, and verify RTL. The goal is to make hardware-design agents more reliable by closing the loop between reasoning and tool feedback.

### Efficient LLM Inference
I study practical ways to reduce inference cost, including KV-cache compression and token-reduction strategies for long-context generation.
